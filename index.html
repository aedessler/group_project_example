Both curves are normal distributions with a standard deviation of 3.0 °C; the present-day mean is 20.0 °C.

A continuous distribution assigns zero probability to any exact value, so "at the target" means within ±0.1 °C of it — the narrow shaded slab, integrated by Simpson's rule. The likelihood factor is essentially independent of that band width: for a pure mean shift it equals exp((2(T−μ)·Δ − Δ²) / 2σ²), which is why the number barely moves when you change the band.
